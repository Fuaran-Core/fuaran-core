# A store load's line scan — 2026-11-19 — Core Ultra 9 386H — Phase 610

Phase 610 asked whether `Jsonl.scanRecords`, the record loop every op-stream reader shares, pays for a full copy of
the text before it scans a line (`text.Replace("\r\n", "\n").Split('\n')`), and whether each op's raw value is copied
a second time before the decoder reads it. The review that filed it estimated the cost from reading. This file is the
measurement, before and after, by the harness committed beside it.

- **Machine:** Intel Core Ultra 9 386H, 16 logical processors, Windows 10.0.26300. Two other benchmarking sessions
  shared the machine; every timed run below was taken holding a shared benchmark lock, so no other measurement
  ran at the same time, but builds and test runs of other sessions may have. The time columns are therefore indicative.
  The allocation and gen2 columns are the measure the phase is about, and they do not depend on load.
- **.NET:** SDK 10.0.300 (the repository's `global.json` floor 10.0.203, rolled forward), Release build of the
  libraries the harness references.
- **Harness:** [`jsonl-load-phase-610.fsx`](jsonl-load-phase-610.fsx), run as its header says, one case per process
  (`dotnet fsi --optimize+ benchmarks/results/jsonl-load-phase-610.fsx -- <store dir>`). The input is built outside
  the timed closure; each case warms for three seconds of wall time, then samples for ten (at least five samples).
  Each case checks its answer (the record or node count) before it is timed.
- **Input:** a production DAG op-stream store, frozen as a copy before the first run: 11 lane files, 9,844 lines,
  30,493,982 bytes, 30,446,365 characters once concatenated (one `\n` between files). Its lines end in `\n` alone. The
  CRLF cases rewrite every `\n` as `\r\n` outside the timed closure, which is the text a checkout that converts line
  endings hands the reader.
- **Cases:** `scan` is `Jsonl.scanRecords` with a decoder that takes the `op` member's raw span (`Jsonl.rawField`),
  the line scan and the member scan and nothing a domain adds. `dag` is `Dag.fromJsonl` with a witness whose op is
  its raw text, the structural load a DAG store's commands start with.
- **Trees:** before, `phase-610` at `0feef93f` (the slot's base); after, the branch at `bb642f71`.
- **Columns:** wall time per call in milliseconds (mean, median, p95), bytes the calling thread allocated per call,
  and generation-2 collections per call.

## Reading it

- **A CRLF text was copied whole once more than an LF text, and no longer is.** Before, the CRLF scan allocated 243.7 MB
  against the LF scan's 182.8 MB: the 60.9 MB between them is the `Replace` copy (30.4 million UTF-16 characters), and
  it moved the load into generation 2 about once per call (0.91 and 1.00 against 0.18 and 0.30). After, a CRLF text
  costs what an LF text costs, byte for byte to within the 9,854 extra characters, and gen2 falls to 0.19 and 0.26 per
  call: a quarter of the CRLF load's bytes and three quarters of its gen2 collections are gone.
- **On an LF text the premise does not hold, and nothing material moved.** `String.Replace` returns its receiver when
  the text holds no `\r\n`, so an LF store was never copied whole before the split. The split's per-line strings are
  the one copy of the lines that any scan returning line strings must make; finding the lines by `IndexOf` makes the
  same copies. What it saves on an LF text is the split's array of 9,844 references (79 KB of 182.8 MB) and the
  `Replace` search pass, which is about what the median time moved by (117.7 to 96.6 ms on the scan; within the
  noise on the DAG load).
- **The second copy, the op's raw value, stays, and a span would not remove it.** `membersOf` copies each member's
  raw value out of the line, and the op's value is most of each line. Every
  route that reads it keeps it as a string: `Jsonl.rawField` and `StreamWitness.Decode` take and return `string` (the
  public contract), and the carrying witness the loads read through (`Jsonl.carry`) holds the raw text beside the
  decoded op, so an op the witness refuses can be hashed and re-encoded byte for byte. Handing the decoder a span would
  still materialise that string, and `Jsonl` is Fable-clean (it runs under both pipelines), where `ReadOnlySpan` does
  not exist. Removing the copy would mean scanning members in place over the whole text (the line copy, not the value
  copy, is the redundant one), which threads an end bound through the strict scanner whose refusal positions the
  `OpStreamLoad` model proves; that is a larger change than the cost it would buy on a load that already runs in a
  tenth of a second over 30 MB.
- **Nothing a reader sees moved.** The lines, their 1-based numbers over blank lines, and every refusal and its
  position are the split's: the suite holds the new scan against the old one, kept verbatim, over every text of up to
  five fragments (terminators of both kinds, a lone `\r`, blank and whitespace lines, a read line, a line the reader
  refuses and a line the scanner refuses), with a go-red that a scan keeping the `\r` of a CRLF fails. The
  `OpStreamLoad` model's differential is unchanged and green.
