# The DAG walks over a whole store — 2026-11-19 — Core Ultra 9 386H — Phase 614

Phase 614 asked whether `Dag.heads` (three whole-DAG lists, a `Set` and a sort per call) and the Kahn drain under
`Dag.between`, `Dag.tryReplayTo` and `Dag.totalOrderBy` (immutable maps rebuilt per node and per edge) cost enough
on a real store to be worth changing. The review that filed it estimated the cost from reading. This file is the
measurement, before and after, by the harness committed beside it.

- **Threshold, written before the baseline run:** change the code only if `Dag.heads` costs at least 0.5 ms median
  or 1 MB allocated per call, or a drain-based call at least 2 ms median or 2 MB allocated per call, on the store
  below; keep the change only if it cuts the allocated bytes per call of the call it targets by at least 20 %, with
  every answer unchanged. The baseline is far above both bounds, so the change was made.
- **Machine:** Intel Core Ultra 9 386H, 16 logical processors, Windows 10.0.26300. Other benchmarking sessions
  shared the machine; every timed run below was taken holding a shared benchmark lock, so no other measurement ran
  at the same time, but builds and test runs of other sessions may have. The time columns are therefore indicative.
  The allocation and gen2 columns are the measure the phase is about, and they do not depend on load.
- **.NET:** SDK 10.0.203, Release build of the libraries the harness references.
- **Harness:** [`dag-walks-phase-614.fsx`](dag-walks-phase-614.fsx), run as its header says, one case per process
  (`dotnet fsi --optimize+ benchmarks/results/dag-walks-phase-614.fsx -- <store dir>`). The DAG, the head and the
  base are built outside the timed closure; each case warms for three seconds of wall time, then samples for eight
  (at least five samples). Each case checks its answer before it is timed.
- **Input:** a production DAG op-stream store, frozen as a copy before the first run: 11 lane files, 30,525,909
  bytes, loaded by `Dag.fromJsonl` into 9,866 nodes with one head (its writers' lanes had converged).
- **Cases:** `Dag.heads`; `Dag.between` from the head's first parent to the head (the delta a fast-forward replays,
  which drains the head's whole closure and walks the base's); `Dag.tryReplayTo` to the head with a witness that
  counts the ops it applies, so the cost is the drain and one `Apply` per node; `Dag.totalOrderBy` at a constant key,
  the drain of the whole DAG.
- **Trees:** before, `phase-614` at `7c12c75e` (the harness alone over the slot); after, the branch at `4db279aa`.
- **Columns:** wall time per call in milliseconds (mean, median, p95), bytes the calling thread allocated per call,
  and generation-2 collections per call.

## Reading it

- **`Dag.heads` allocates 2.4 % of what it did.** 8.33 MB per call becomes 0.20 MB, which is the `HashSet` of
  parent ids and the one-element answer; the median falls from 4.7 ms to 1.1 ms. The parents are gathered in one
  walk of the node map and the heads read off it with a right fold. The map's keys are already in ordinal order,
  which is the order `List.sort` gave, so the sort is gone with no change to the answer.
- **The whole-DAG drain allocates 12 % of what it did.** `Dag.totalOrderBy` falls from 33.8 MB to 4.2 MB per call
  and from 32 ms to 9 ms median. What remains is the ready frontier, which stays an ordered `Set` because it defines
  the order, the two `Dictionary`s, and the answer lists.
- **`between` and `tryReplayTo` allocate 47 % and 39 % of what they did** (55.4 to 25.8 MB, 48.4 to 18.8 MB). The
  drain's share of them fell as the whole-DAG drain's did; the rest is the ancestor closure, which this phase did
  not change. `closureOf` builds the closure as an immutable `Set` and copies each node's parent list onto its work
  list, and the closure drain rebuilds the node map restricted to that set (`Map.filter`) before it drains it;
  `between` walks a second closure for its base. Those are about 14.6 MB of the 18.8 MB a replay to the head now
  allocates. Removing them means changing the closure walk that `ancestorsOf` publishes as a `Set`, so it is
  reported here as the next cost on this path rather than changed under this phase's name.
- **The structures are sized to the node count, because growing them cost gen2 collections.** A first after-run
  with the `HashSet` and `Dictionary`s growing by doubling allocated 2.0 MB for `heads` (0.67 MB once the map's own
  iterators replaced its enumerator) and rose to 0.03 to 0.04 gen2 collections per call on the drain-based calls,
  against 0.000 to 0.009 before: at ten thousand nodes each structure ends above the large-object threshold, and
  each doubling past it left another large array behind. Sized once, each allocates one such array, and the gen2
  rate is back within the baseline's range (0.005 to 0.012 per call, from at most a few collections per case).
- **Nothing a reader sees moved.** The drained order, the unplaced nodes of a cycle in id order, and the heads in
  ordinal order are held against the previous implementations, kept verbatim in the suite, over 600 hand-built
  DAGs with repeated parents, dangling parents, self-loops and cycles, at a constant key and a domain key, for the
  whole DAG and for every node's closure; a drain that takes the largest ready node instead disagrees over the same
  sample, so the comparison can fail, and each of the two comparisons goes red when the production code is
  perturbed (a reversed heads fold; a largest-first frontier). The DagFold and DagClosure differentials in the
  proof oracle family are green on the changed tree, and the models do not move.
